05Discussion


Observations on the toy setup. The group order is small, so points repeat with period n: for p = 17, the curve has 18 points (including the point at infinity), meaning k and k+n yield the same point Q and the discrete logarithm problem is trivially solvable via brute force. Real security relies solely on n ≈ 2²⁵⁶.


Meaningful properties for a one-way function (ECDLP): determinism; fast forward computation (double-and-add, ~log k steps); hardness of inversion; "seemingly uniform" output over the curve points (no exploitable information about k from the distributions of x, y, or individual bits); absence of exploitable correlation between k and Q in practice.


Typical hash function properties not required (and absent) here: the avalanche effect and collision/second-preimage resistance for arbitrary-length inputs with fixed-size outputs. The mapping kG is a homomorphism: (k₁+k₂)G = k₁G + k₂G, hence Q(k+1) = Q(k) + G. Flipping a single bit of k does NOT flip half of the bits of Q at random; this algebraic structure is hidden only by the hardness of the DLP, and this very structure enables digital signatures and ECDH. A hash function, in contrast, must destroy any algebraic structure.


In summary: uniformity and non-invertibility are relevant to both; avalanche and collision resistance are hash-specific properties; linearity (homomorphism) is a deliberate and essential feature of ECC that hash functions must not possess.
