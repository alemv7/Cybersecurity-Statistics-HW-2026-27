HW 3 · Penetration scores: LLN and CLT

Alessandro Bertoldi · Statistics in Cybersecurity. n systems, m attackers: each attacker is a random trajectory of i.i.d. scores X₁…Xₙ, and we study Sₙ, Sₙ/n and Sₙ/√n as n grows.

01Model and what to expect

  • Setup. Attacker a goes through n systems; Xᵢ is the score obtained on system i. Bernoulli: Xᵢ ∈ {0,1}, P(1) = p (system compromised or not), μ = p, σ² = p(1−p). Rademacher: Xᵢ = ±1 with prob. ½ (symmetric progress/setback), μ = 0, σ² = 1.
  • Total score Sₙ: E = nμ, Var = nσ². Spread grows like √n.
  • Average Sₙ/n: E = μ, Var = σ²/n → 0. LLN: P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) → 0 (convergence in probability). Chebyshev gives σ²/(nε²), Hoeffding an exponential bound.
  • Normalized total Sₙ/√n: Var = σ² for every n, so the fluctuations neither vanish nor explode. CLT: (Sₙ − nμ)/(σ√n) → N(0,1) in distribution. For Rademacher μ = 0, so Sₙ/√n itself → N(0,1). For Bernoulli Sₙ/√n has mean μ√n → ∞ and only its centered version converges.

02Simulation

03Interpretation: n systems, m attackers

  • System size n. For the Bernoulli model the total number of compromised systems grows linearly, ≈ np, but its uncertainty only like √(np(1−p)); the fraction compromised Sₙ/n therefore concentrates at p: for large n the aggregate outcome becomes predictable (LLN) even though each single system is random. In the symmetric Rademacher case the average score goes to 0, yet the total wanders at the scale √n; Sₙ/√n shows this scale is stable and Gaussian (CLT), which gives confidence intervals such as Sₙ ∈ ±2σ√n with about 95% probability.
  • Number of attackers m. m does not change the law of any single attacker's score. It improves how precisely we can estimate the distribution (error ≈ 1/√m, see the KS critical value) and it drives the worst case: among m independent attackers the best one reaches about nμ + σ√(2n ln m), so extreme outcomes grow with √ln m while the typical one does not. The total over all attackers scales like m·n·μ in the mean.
  • Variance under each normalization. Var(Sₙ) = nσ² (grows), Var(Sₙ/n) = σ²/n (vanishes, concentration), Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ² (constant, the non-degenerate limit).
  • Limits of the model. Real systems are not i.i.d.: shared vulnerabilities create positive correlation (slower concentration, larger variance), heavy-tailed scores can break the CLT, attackers adapt, and p varies across systems. The lattice nature of Sₙ also makes the Gaussian approximation visibly coarse for small n (see the continuity-corrected KS column).
